Section A — Multiple Choice Questions 20 × 1 mark = 20 marks
Question 1 1 mark
A family which consists of mother, father, children and paternal or maternal relatives living together in the same residence is called:
✔ Correct Answer
c) Extended family
Why? A nuclear family has only parents and children. An extended family also includes relatives like grandparents, aunts, uncles, or cousins living in the same home.
Question 2 1 mark
Which of the following is an advantage of an extended family over a nuclear family?
✔ Correct Answer
b) Labour is easily available for household chores
Why? Because there are more members in an extended family, tasks like cooking, farming and childcare can be shared among many people.
Question 3 1 mark
The pride you develop about your better qualities, superior behavioural patterns and capabilities is known as:
✔ Correct Answer
b) Self-esteem
Why? Self-esteem is specifically defined in the textbook as the pride developed in oneself about better qualities, superior behavioural patterns and special capabilities. It is different from self-confidence (belief in ability) or self-discipline (control of behaviour).
Question 4 1 mark
Which of the following is a Civil Right?
✔ Correct Answer
c) Right to follow any religion
Why? Civil rights include: right to live, right to education, right to follow any religion, right to expression, right to employment, equality, and right to live in a preferred place. The right to vote and the right to criticize the government are political rights.
Question 5 1 mark
DNA stands for:
✔ Correct Answer
b) Deoxyribonucleic Acid
Why? DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid) structure differs from person to person and is used to identify individuals uniquely — this is mentioned in the textbook under the topic of "Your Identity".
Question 6 1 mark
According to Sri Lankan law, children below the age of _____ cannot be employed:
✔ Correct Answer
b) 14
Why? According to the Shop and Office Employees Act in Sri Lanka, children below 14 years of age cannot be employed. Since education is compulsory, every child under 14 should be in school.
Question 7 1 mark
Government servants in Sri Lanka are required to retire at the age of:
✔ Correct Answer
b) 57
Why? Government servants must retire at 57 but have the option to continue until 60. Private sector servants receive their EPF at 55.
Question 8 1 mark
In which era of human civilisation did people work cooperatively to hunt for food and fulfill basic needs?
✔ Correct Answer
c) Hunting era
Why? In the hunting era, people lived a collective life and worked together to hunt for food and meet basic needs. The nomadic era came next, when people moved in search of grasslands for animal rearing.
Question 9 1 mark
The emergency call bureau number mentioned in the Civics textbook is:
✔ Correct Answer
b) 119
Why? The textbook specifically mentions: 119 = Emergency call bureau; 1919 = State information centre. Remember: 119 is the emergency number (like 911 in some countries).
Question 10 1 mark
Which of the following is a Political Right?
✔ Correct Answer
c) Right to be an elector
Why? Political rights include: right to be an elector (voter), right to be a candidate, joining any political party, electing any government, criticizing the government, and holding government posts. Rights to education, life, and religion are civil rights.
Question 11 1 mark
The smallest social unit in society is:
✔ Correct Answer
c) Family
Why? The textbook states clearly: "Family is the smallest social unit in a society." The society is made up of a collection of family units.
Question 12 1 mark
Customs and rituals that have prevailed in society for a long time are called:
✔ Correct Answer
b) Traditions
Why? Customs guide us on what to do and avoid in specific occasions. Traditions are customs and rituals that have prevailed in society for a long time — they are deeper and longer lasting.
Question 13 1 mark
Which of the following activities helps to control unfavourable emotions?
✔ Correct Answer
b) Engaging in religious activities
Why? The textbook lists religious activities, walking, reading books, listening to radio, watching meaningful TV programmes, and engaging in sports as activities that help control unfavourable emotions.
Question 14 1 mark
Which of the following information must be printed on packaged food products?
✔ Correct Answer
c) Expiry date
Why? The textbook lists five compulsory items on product labels: Date of Manufacture, Expiry Date, Maximum Retail Price, Net Weight, and Ingredients. "Country of manufacture" and "name of the shop" are NOT required.
Question 15 1 mark
Private sector female employees can obtain their Employees' Provident Fund (EPF):
✔ Correct Answer
b) At age 55 or upon marriage
Why? The textbook states: "Private sector servants can get their Employees Provident Fund at the age of 55. However, female employees can get it for their marriage." This means female workers have an additional option — they can withdraw it when they get married.
Question 16 1 mark
Which of the following is NOT a challenge faced by modern families?
✔ Correct Answer
d) Having many helpful neighbours
Why? The textbook lists challenges like parents working abroad, differences in attitudes, insufficient income, and busy lifestyles. Helpful neighbours are a support — they help a family overcome challenges, not create them.
Question 17 1 mark
'Data' in the context of the Civics lesson means:
✔ Correct Answer
b) Sources which provide information related to a subject
Why? The textbook defines data as "sources which provide information related to a scope of a subject." Data is not limited to the internet — it includes our personal experiences, newspapers, radio, etc.
Question 18 1 mark
Which of the following is NOT listed as a great quality for a good citizen?
✔ Correct Answer
c) Selfishness
Why? The textbook lists "Selflessness / Altruism" as a great quality — meaning thinking of others before yourself. Selfishness is the opposite and is NOT a desirable quality for a good citizen.
Question 19 1 mark
Article 23 of the Universal Charter on Human Rights gives every individual:
✔ Correct Answer
b) The right to work and choose one's occupation freely
Why? Article 23 specifically gives the right to work, right to choose one's occupation freely, right to enjoy reasonable facilities while working, and to ensure job security. This is the article that protects workers' rights.
Question 20 1 mark
Which of the following qualities is important for facing challenges successfully?
✔ Correct Answer
b) Self-confidence and firm determination
Why? The textbook emphasises that challenges must be faced — not avoided. Qualities needed include self-confidence, firm determination, tolerance, creativity, bravery, and rationality.

Question 23 2 marks
In the story of Little Shyama, what qualities did Shyama show that reflected her self-esteem? Name TWO.
✔ Model Answer
  • Generosity / Compassion — She gave all her saved money (Rs. 120) to the helpless grandfather without hesitation.
  • Habit of saving — She had saved money responsibly from amounts given to her.
  • Selflessness — She chose to help a stranger in need rather than buy something for herself.

(1 mark per quality)
Remember: Self-esteem comes from acting on your good values. Shyama's pride came from doing the right thing — not from buying the ring!

Section C — Structured Questions 4 × 5 marks = 20 marks
Question 31 5 marks
(a) Distinguish between a nuclear family and an extended family. (2 marks)
(b) Explain THREE ways in which being part of an extended family benefits its members. (3 marks)
✔ Model Answer
(a) (2 marks)
A nuclear family consists only of the mother, father and their children living together in one home.
An extended family consists of the mother, father, children and some paternal or maternal relatives living together in the same home.
The key difference is that the extended family includes additional relatives beyond just parents and children.

(b) Any THREE of the following, with brief explanation (1 mark each):
  • Greater family strength — More members means the family is stronger and can handle difficulties more easily.
  • Shared household labour — Routine household chores and economic activities can be shared among many members, making them easier to complete.
  • More love and affection for children — Children receive love, protection and affection not just from parents but from grandparents, uncles, aunts and cousins as well.
  • Passing down culture — Younger generations learn customs, traditions and cultural practices directly from elders in the home, helping to preserve them.
  • Shared responsibilities — Because responsibilities are distributed among many members, family affairs are managed more easily and conveniently.
  • Broader social relationships — Members have the opportunity to build wider relationships with the external society through extended family networks.
Marking note: For full marks, the student should clearly distinguish the two family types and give THREE specific, explained benefits — not just list words.
Question 32 5 marks
(a) What is meant by the term 'rights'? (1 mark)
(b) List THREE civil rights available to citizens in society. (3 marks)
(c) State ONE duty that is linked to the right to education. (1 mark)
✔ Model Answer
(a) Rights are the privileges given to a person by the human society that are important to make life satisfying and allow a person to live a good and dignified life. (1 mark)

(b) Any THREE civil rights: (1 mark each)
  • Right to live
  • Right to education
  • Right to follow any religion or faith
  • Right to expression
  • Right to employment
  • Equality
  • Right to live in a place of preference
(c) The duty linked to the right to education is: to engage in educational activities with enthusiasm, be obedient to teachers, follow their instructions, and strive to get a good education in order to serve society as a good citizen. (1 mark)
Key lesson: Every right comes with a matching duty. If you have the right to education, your duty is to take it seriously. If you have the right to live, your duty is to live without bothering others.
Question 33 5 marks
(a) How did human society evolve from simple to complex? Briefly describe FOUR stages. (4 marks)
(b) What does this evolution tell us about the importance of the family unit in society? (1 mark)
✔ Model Answer
(a) Four stages of societal evolution: (1 mark each)

  1. Hunting Era — People lived a collective life and worked together to hunt animals and gather food to fulfill their basic needs. Society was very simple.
  2. Nomadic Era — People moved from place to place in search of grasslands for animal rearing. They began to exchange animal products and work in larger groups.
  3. Agrarian Era — With the development of agriculture, people settled down. They worked together to prepare land, cultivate and harvest crops, and exchange goods and services. A peasant society developed.
  4. Industrial / Technological Era — Society became more complex as industrial knowledge grew. Goods were produced, resources extracted, trade expanded, and eventually the modern technological era emerged with even greater complexity.

(b) Throughout all these stages, the family remained the smallest and most fundamental unit of society. As society grew more complex, the family unit also changed — but it always remained the foundation on which the entire society was built. (1 mark)
Memory aid: Hunting → Nomadic → Agrarian → Industrial/Technological = HNAI (Hunting Nomads Are Industrious!)
Question 34 5 marks
(a) What is child labour? (1 mark)
(b) State THREE negative impacts of child labour on a child's life. (3 marks)
(c) Describe ONE action that society can take to prevent child labour. (1 mark)
✔ Model Answer
(a) Child labour refers to the employment of children (those below the legal working age of 14 in Sri Lanka) to perform work — which deprives them of their right to education, childhood, and healthy development. (1 mark)

(b) Any THREE negative impacts: (1 mark each)
  • Physical and mental strength weakens — children fall ill
  • Exploitation of their labour (they are often underpaid or unpaid)
  • Subject to abuse and harassment
  • Losing opportunities for education and learning
  • Losing the pleasant childhood they deserve
  • Engagement in anti-social activities
  • Addiction to intoxicated drugs
  • Getting physical or mental disorders
(c) Any ONE of the following actions: (1 mark)
  • Treating all children as our own and directing them to school for compulsory education
  • Not employing children for narrow personal expectations
  • Reporting child labour to the Commissioner of Labour or relevant institution
  • Adults not depending on children financially
  • Raising awareness among parents about the harmful effects of child labour
Important law to remember: The Shop and Office Employees Act prohibits employing children below 14. Violations should be reported to the Commissioner of Labour.
