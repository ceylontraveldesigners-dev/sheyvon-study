⚠️ Natural Hazards and Disasters

Natural Hazards, Disasters & Their Impact in Sri Lanka

📖 Key Concepts

Hazard — A natural phenomenon that may cause damage to community or property. It is a continuous process.
Disaster — When a hazard actually affects the community or property and causes loss.
Drought — Extended period with very low rainfall. Causes water scarcity, crop failure, epidemics.
Flood — Overflow of rivers, canals and streams due to excess rainfall. Common in low-lying areas.
Earth Slip (Landslide) — Movement of soil and rocks on hillsides, mainly in hilly areas during heavy rain.
Cyclone — Tropical storm with very strong rotating winds. Affects coastal areas.
Tsunami — Giant waves caused by underwater earthquakes. Hit Sri Lanka severely in 2004.
Lightning — Electrical discharge between clouds and ground during storms.

📌 Important Rules & Facts

📌 A hazard becomes a disaster only when it causes harm to people or property.
📌 Droughts are made worse by deforestation, global warming and removal of vegetation.
📌 Floods occur more in urban areas due to blocked drains and removal of vegetation.
📌 Earth slips are most common in hilly regions during heavy rainfall.
📌 Most hazards in Sri Lanka are worsening due to unfavourable human activities.

💡 Examples

2004 Tsunami: A major underwater earthquake near Indonesia caused a devastating tsunami that hit Sri Lanka's eastern and southern coasts, killing over 30,000 people.
Flood-prone districts: Districts like Ratnapura, Kalutara and Colombo experience regular flooding during the southwest monsoon.
Drought-prone districts: Hambantota, Vavuniya and Mullaitivu are frequently affected by droughts in the Dry Zone.

🔑 Chapter Summary

A hazard is a natural risk; it becomes a disaster when it harms people or property. Sri Lanka faces droughts, floods, earth slips, cyclones, tsunamis, lightning and earthquakes. Human activities like deforestation worsen most hazards. Being prepared reduces the impact of disasters.

🌟 Bonus Questions

Q: Give three causes of droughts in Sri Lanka.
A: Decrease of rainfall; deforestation; increase of global warming.
Q: What is the difference between a hazard and a disaster?
A: A hazard is a natural phenomenon that may cause damage. It becomes a disaster only when it actually harms people or property.
