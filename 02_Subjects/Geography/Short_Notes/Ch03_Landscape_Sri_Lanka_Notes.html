🏔️ The Landscape of Sri Lanka

Hill Country, Dry Zone, Wet Zone, Jaffna Peninsula & Coastal Belt

📖 Key Concepts

Hill Country — Lands above 300 m. Found in Central Hills and Sabaragamuwa Hills. Tea cultivation, waterfalls, mountain ranges.
Dry Zone — North, North-central, East, Southeast. Low, uncertain rainfall. Ancient Sri Lankan civilisation began here.
Wet Zone — Southwest Sri Lanka. High rainfall. Rubber, coconut, rice, spices cultivation. Dense forests.
Jaffna Peninsula — Northern flat limestone peninsula. Tobacco, onions, chilli farming. Kankesanthurai harbour.
Coastal Belt — Surrounds the island. Sandy beaches, lagoons, mangroves. Fishing communities. Tourism.
Residual Hills — Isolated hills in the Dry Zone (e.g., Sigiriya rock). Left after erosion.

📌 Important Rules & Facts

📌 The Hill Country landscape is above 300 m above mean sea level.
📌 The Dry Zone has severe drought, high temperature, and low, uncertain rainfall.
📌 The Wet Zone receives most of Sri Lanka's rainfall (especially the southwest monsoon).
📌 Jaffna Peninsula is a flat limestone plateau — fertile for vegetables.
📌 Sri Lanka's coastline is approximately 1,340 km long.

💡 Examples

Hill Country feature: Tea estates cover the slopes of Nuwara Eliya — a key Hill Country landscape feature.
Dry Zone feature: Ancient reservoirs (tanks) like Parakrama Samudra were built to store water for farming in the Dry Zone.
Wet Zone feature: Rubber estates and dense rain forests dominate Ratnapura district in the Wet Zone.

🔑 Chapter Summary

Sri Lanka's landscapes include the Hill Country (>300 m, tea), Dry Zone (low rainfall, ancient civilisation), Wet Zone (high rainfall, rubber/coconut), Jaffna Peninsula (flat, limestone, vegetables), and Coastal Belt (beaches, lagoons, fishing). Each region has unique physical and human features.

🌟 Bonus Questions

Q: Give two physical features of the Hill Country landscape.
A: Mountain ranges and plateaus; rivers flowing through valleys and waterfalls.
Q: Why did the ancient Sri Lankan civilisation develop in the Dry Zone?
A: The flat terrain and rivers like Mahaweli allowed construction of large reservoirs for irrigation farming.
