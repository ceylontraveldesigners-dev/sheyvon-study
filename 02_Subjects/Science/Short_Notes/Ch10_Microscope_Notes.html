📌 Key Concepts

Simple Microscope: Uses ONE lens. E.g. magnifying glass. Limited magnification.
Compound Microscope: Uses TWO or more lenses (eyepiece + objective). Higher magnification.
Eyepiece Lens: The lens you look through at the top of the compound microscope.
Objective Lens: The lens close to the specimen. Usually 3 objective lenses (4×, 10×, 40×).
Magnification: Total magnification = eyepiece power × objective power.
Resolving Power: The ability to distinguish two closely spaced points as separate.

📏 Key Rules & Facts

✅ Total magnification = eyepiece lens power × objective lens power.
✅ Always start with the lowest power objective lens.
✅ Use coarse adjustment first, then fine adjustment to focus.
✅ Electron microscopes have far greater magnification than light microscopes.
✅ The slide is placed on the stage and held with stage clips.

🔬 Real-Life Examples

🔬 Eyepiece 10× and objective 40× → total magnification = 400×.
🔬 Coarse adjustment knob: for initial large focus changes.
🔬 Fine adjustment knob: for precise focusing at high magnification.
🔬 Electron microscope used to view viruses — too small for light microscope.
