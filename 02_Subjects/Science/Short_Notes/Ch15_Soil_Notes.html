📌 Key Concepts

Sandy Soil: Large particles, drains quickly, low nutrient content. Gritty texture.
Clay Soil: Very fine particles, holds water well, waterlogged easily. Sticky when wet.
Loam Soil: Mix of sand, silt, and clay. Best for plants. Holds moisture but drains well.
Humus: Decomposed organic matter in soil — provides nutrients for plants.
Soil Erosion: The removal of topsoil by wind or water.
Soil Conservation: Practices to prevent erosion, e.g. terracing, tree planting.

📏 Key Rules & Facts

✅ Loam soil is best for growing most plants — good balance of drainage and nutrients.
✅ Sandy soil drains too quickly; clay soil retains too much water.
✅ Humus is formed from dead plants and animals broken down by decomposers.
✅ Soil erosion removes the fertile topsoil — preventing it is vital for farming.
✅ Plant roots hold soil and prevent erosion.

🔬 Real-Life Examples

🔬 Sandy soil — found on beaches; dries out quickly.
🔬 Clay soil — very sticky, cracks when dry, used for bricks and pottery.
🔬 Loam soil — found in fertile farmland; best for growing rice, vegetables.
🔬 Terracing on hillsides — prevents soil being washed away by rain.
