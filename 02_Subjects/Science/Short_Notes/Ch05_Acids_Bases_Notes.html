📌 Key Concepts

Acid: A substance with pH below 7. Turns blue litmus red. Tastes sour.
Base: A substance with pH above 7. Turns red litmus blue. Feels slippery.
Neutral: pH = 7. Neither acid nor base. E.g. pure water.
pH Scale: A scale from 0–14 measuring how acidic or basic a substance is.
Indicator: A substance that changes colour to show if something is an acid or base.
Neutralisation: Reaction between an acid and a base to form a salt and water.

📏 Key Rules & Facts

✅ Acids have pH < 7; bases have pH > 7; neutral substances have pH = 7.
✅ Litmus paper: acid turns blue litmus RED; base turns red litmus BLUE.
✅ Universal indicator shows a range of colours across the pH scale.
✅ Acids taste sour (lemon, vinegar); bases feel slippery (soap, baking soda).
✅ Acid + Base → Salt + Water (neutralisation).

🔬 Real-Life Examples

🔬 Lemon juice — acid (pH ≈ 2), turns blue litmus red.
🔬 Baking soda solution — base (pH ≈ 9), turns red litmus blue.
🔬 Pure water — neutral (pH = 7).
🔬 Soap — base; vinegar — acid; stomach acid (HCl) — strong acid.
