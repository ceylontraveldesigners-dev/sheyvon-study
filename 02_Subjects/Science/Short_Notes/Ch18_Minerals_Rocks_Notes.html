📌 Key Concepts

Igneous Rock: Formed from cooled magma/lava. E.g. granite, basalt.
Sedimentary Rock: Formed from layers of sediment compressed over time. E.g. sandstone, limestone.
Metamorphic Rock: Formed when existing rock is changed by heat and pressure. E.g. marble, slate.
Rock Cycle: The continuous process by which rocks change from one type to another.
Weathering: The breakdown of rocks by physical, chemical, or biological means.
Mineral: A naturally occurring, inorganic solid with a definite chemical composition. E.g. quartz, feldspar.

📏 Key Rules & Facts

✅ Three rock types: igneous, sedimentary, metamorphic.
✅ Igneous: from magma/lava; sedimentary: from compressed sediment; metamorphic: from heat and pressure.
✅ Weathering breaks rocks into smaller pieces — physical, chemical, or biological.
✅ The rock cycle shows how rocks constantly change from one type to another.
✅ Minerals are the building blocks of rocks.

🔬 Real-Life Examples

🔬 Granite — igneous rock formed from slow-cooling magma underground.
🔬 Limestone — sedimentary rock formed from shells and coral.
🔬 Marble — metamorphic rock formed from limestone under heat and pressure.
🔬 Rust forming on iron — chemical weathering.
