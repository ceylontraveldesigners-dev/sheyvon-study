🔑 Key Concepts — Tessellation

Tessellation: A pattern of shapes that fit together perfectly without gaps or overlaps, covering a flat surface.
Regular Tessellation: Using one type of regular polygon. Only equilateral triangles, squares, and regular hexagons tessellate regularly.
Semi-regular Tessellation: Using two or more types of regular polygons.
Condition for Tessellation: Angles meeting at each vertex must add up to exactly 360°.
Interior Angle: The angle inside a regular polygon. For n-sided polygon: interior angle = (n−2)×180÷n.

📌 Rules to Remember

✅ Rule 1: Shapes tessellate only if their angles at a vertex add up to exactly 360°.
✅ Rule 2: Only 3 regular polygons can tessellate on their own: equilateral triangle (60°), square (90°), regular hexagon (120°). 60×6=360, 90×4=360, 120×3=360.
❌ Common Mistake: Regular pentagons do NOT tessellate (interior angle = 108°; 360÷108 is not a whole number).

✏️ Worked Examples

Example 1: Does a square tessellate?

→ Interior angle=90°. 360÷90=4. Yes, 4 squares meet at each vertex. ✓
Example 2: Does a regular hexagon tessellate?

→ Interior angle=120°. 360÷120=3. Yes, 3 hexagons meet. ✓
Example 3: Does a regular pentagon tessellate?

→ Interior angle=108°. 360÷108≈3.33. Not a whole number. ✗
