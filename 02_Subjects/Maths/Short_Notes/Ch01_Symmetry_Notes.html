🔑 What is Bilateral Symmetry?

Bilateral Symmetry: A plane figure has bilateral symmetry if it can be folded along a straight line so that the two parts exactly coincide (fit perfectly on top of each other).
Axis of Symmetry: The straight line along which a bilaterally symmetric figure is folded is called an axis of symmetry. It is the fold line.

Think of it like folding a piece of paper — if both halves match up perfectly, the fold line is the axis of symmetry.

📌 Key Rules to Remember

✅ Rule 1: In a bilaterally symmetric figure, the two parts on either side of the axis of symmetry have the same shape, same area, AND they coincide.
❌ Common Mistake: A line that divides a figure into two equal AREAS does NOT automatically make it an axis of symmetry. The two parts must also coincide exactly when folded.

Example: A diagonal line through a rectangle divides it into two triangles of equal area — but they do NOT coincide. So the diagonal is NOT an axis of symmetry of the rectangle.
✅ Rule 2: A figure can have more than one axis of symmetry. A square has 4, a circle has infinitely many!

🧠 Memory Tip: For a regular polygon, the number of axes of symmetry = the number of sides.
Square (4 sides) → 4 axes | Equilateral triangle (3 sides) → 3 axes | Regular pentagon (5 sides) → 5 axes

✏️ Worked Examples

Example 1: How many axes of symmetry does a regular pentagon have?

A regular pentagon has 5 equal sides.
Number of axes = number of sides = 5 axes of symmetry.
Example 2: Does a rectangle have 4 axes of symmetry?

No! A rectangle has only 2 axes of symmetry — one horizontal (through the middle) and one vertical (through the middle).
The diagonals of a rectangle do NOT create two coinciding halves, so they are NOT axes of symmetry.
(A square DOES have 4 axes because its diagonals also create two coinciding halves.)
Example 3: A circle and a square — which has more axes of symmetry?

A circle has infinitely many axes (any diameter is an axis).
A square has only 4 axes.
So a circle has more axes of symmetry than a square. ✅

📏 Drawing Symmetric Figures on a Grid

When drawing the symmetric half of a figure on squared paper:

  • Identify the axis of symmetry (the fold line).
  • For each vertex (corner point), count how many squares it is from the axis of symmetry.
  • Mark a new point the same number of squares on the OTHER side of the axis.
  • Connect the new points to complete the symmetric figure.
Key Idea: Each point and its corresponding point are always the same distance from the axis of symmetry, on opposite sides, on a line perpendicular to the axis.
