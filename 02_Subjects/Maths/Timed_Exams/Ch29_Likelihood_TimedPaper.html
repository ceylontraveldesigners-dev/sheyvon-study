Section A — Multiple Choice (10 marks)

Q1. Cards numbered 1−10. P(prime)?

💡 Primes: 2,3,5,7=4 cards. P=4/10=2/5

Q2. P(not selecting a vowel from A,B,C,D,E)?

💡 Vowels: A,E=2. P(vowel)=2/5. P(not)=3/5. Wait: ABCDE has 2 vowels (A,E). P(not vowel)=3/5

Q3. Two coins tossed. P(two heads)?

💡 HH,HT,TH,TT. P(HH)=1/4

Q4. Bag: 5 red, 3 blue, 2 green. P(not red)?

💡 P(not red)=(3+2)/10=5/10=1/2

Q5. Die rolled. P(factor of 6)?

💡 Factors of 6: 1,2,3,6=4. P=4/6=2/3

Q6. P(picking Tuesday from days of week)?

💡 7 days; 1 is Tuesday.

Q7. If P(A)=0.45, P(B)=0.35, and A,B mutually exclusive. P(A or B)?

💡 0.45+0.35=0.8

Q8. Cards 1−20. P(multiple of 4)?

💡 Multiples of 4: 4,8,12,16,20=5. P=5/20=1/4

Q9. An event is certain. Its probability = ?

💡 Certain = P=1

Q10. P(rolling a number > 4) on standard die?

💡 Numbers>4: 5,6=2. P=2/6=1/3

Section B — True or False (3 marks)

Q11. P = 0.5 means an event is equally likely to happen or not.

💡 0.5 = 50% chance = equally likely.

Q12. The more times you roll a die, the closer the frequency gets to the theoretical probability.

💡 Law of large numbers.

Q13. If P(A) = 1/3, P(A) can also be written as 33.3% or approximately 0.333.

💡 All equivalent representations.

Section C — Fill in the Blank (2 marks)

Q14. Bag: 3 red, 7 blue. P(blue) = ___.

💡 7 out of 10.

Q15. P(certain event) = ___.

💡 Always 1.
