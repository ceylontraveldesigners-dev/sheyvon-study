Question 22 2.5 marks
Describe what happened to Prophet Jonah when he tried to flee from God's call. What lesson can we learn from his story?
Model Answer
God called Jonah to go to Nineveh and preach repentance, but Jonah refused and boarded a ship sailing to Tarshish. God sent a great storm, and the sailors threw Jonah overboard. A huge fish swallowed him and he was inside for three days and three nights. He prayed to God, and the fish spat him out. Jonah then went to Nineveh and the people repented.

Lesson: We cannot run away from God's call. When we are faithful to God's mission, His mercy and salvation can reach all people.
1.5 marks for the events (fleeing → storm → fish → Nineveh). 1 mark for a lesson/moral.
Jonah = "Runaway Prophet." He ran to Tarshish but ended in a fish — God always finds a way!
Question 23 2.5 marks
What were the TWO elements of Abraham's call? Give one specific example of each from the textbook.
Model Answer
Element 1 — Sacrifice: Abraham had to give up his home, his country (Ur and Haran), his relatives and his father's family to go to a land God would show him. This required leaving behind everything familiar.

Element 2 — Challenge: Abraham had to trust God entirely for a journey of about 800 miles to Canaan — a land he had never seen. He had no map or certainty, only faith. The greatest challenge was when God asked him to sacrifice his own son Isaac.
1.25 marks per element (0.75 for naming/explaining, 0.5 for an example).
Sacrifice = giving up things. Challenge = facing the unknown. Abraham did BOTH — that's why he's the "Father of Faith."

Question 25 2.5 marks
Describe what happens at the most important and essential part of the Baptismal Rite. What does it symbolise?
Model Answer
The most important and essential part of the Baptismal Rite is the pouring of water (or immersion in water) over the person's head, while the minister says: "I baptise you in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit."

Symbolism: Water symbolises cleansing from original sin and new life in Christ. It represents the death of the old self and rising to new life — just as Jesus died and rose again.
1 mark for describing the pouring of water with the Trinitarian formula. 1.5 marks for explaining the symbolism (cleansing sin + new life).
Three things happen at Baptism: water (cleansing), white garment (purity), paschal candle (light of Christ). Water is the ESSENTIAL part.

Question 27 2.5 marks
Explain the difference between the 7th commandment (Do Not Steal) and the 10th commandment (Do Not Covet Your Neighbour's Goods). Give one example to show the difference.
Model Answer
The 7th commandment forbids the external act of stealing — physically taking someone else's property without their permission. It is about what we DO.

The 10th commandment forbids the internal desire — coveting, jealousy or greedily desiring what belongs to someone else even if we never steal it. It is about what we WANT or FEEL inside our heart.

Example: If Sheyvon's classmate has a nice new pencil case and Sheyvon steals it — that is breaking the 7th commandment. But if Sheyvon only feels jealous and wants it, even though he does not steal it — that is breaking the 10th commandment.
1 mark for explaining the 7th (external action). 1 mark for explaining the 10th (internal desire/attitude). 0.5 mark for a valid example.
7th = STEALING (action). 10th = COVETING (attitude/desire). You can break the 10th without breaking the 7th!

Question 29 5 marks
The Oratory Prophets: (a) Name the three. (b) Choose ONE and write about his work in detail. (c) Give ONE example of how you can be a "prophet" today.
Model Answer
(a) The three Oratory Prophets are: Nathan, Elijah and Elisha. (1.5 marks)

(b) Example — Elijah: Elijah was one of the most powerful prophets of Israel. He challenged the prophets of Baal to a contest on Mount Carmel to prove that the God of Israel was the true God. God sent fire from heaven to consume Elijah's sacrifice, proving His power. Elijah also showed God's care for the poor by helping the widow of Zarephath, whose jar of flour and oil never ran out, and who he raised her son from the dead. This teaches us that God always protects those who are faithful and that we should stand up for truth even when it is difficult. (2.5 marks)

Example — Nathan: Nathan was the prophet who had the courage to confront King David about his sin of taking Bathsheba and having Uriah killed. He used a parable of a rich man and a poor man's lamb. When David was angry at the rich man, Nathan said "You are that man!" This teaches us that true friends and leaders must speak the truth in love, even to powerful people.

(c) I can be a "prophet" today by speaking up against bullying in school even when others are quiet, or by reminding a friend to be honest when they want to cheat in a test. (1 mark)
1.5 marks for part (a). 2.5 marks for part (b) — must include: who the prophet is, what he did, a specific event, and a lesson. 1 mark for part (c) — must be a concrete example in own life.
Nathan corrected KING David. Elijah challenged 450 Baal prophets. Elisha cured Naaman. All three were brave enough to speak God's truth to powerful people.
Question 30 5 marks
Abraham — Father of Faith: (a) God's two promises. (b) What does "Father of Faith" mean? Two faith examples. (c) One personal example you want to follow.
Model Answer
(a) The two promises God made in the Covenant with Abraham: (2 marks)
Promise 1: Abraham's descendants would be as many as the stars in the sky — he would have a large generation (Genesis 15:5).
Promise 2: God would give him and his descendants the land of Canaan as an everlasting possession (Genesis 17:6).

(b) Abraham is called the "Father of Faith" because he trusted God completely even when it made no human sense and the outcome was impossible to see. He obeyed God without knowing the result. (2 marks)
Faith example 1: When God called Abraham to leave Ur and travel 800 miles to an unknown land, Abraham "went, even though he did not know where he was going" (Hebrews 11:8).
Faith example 2: When God asked Abraham to sacrifice his only son Isaac on Mount Moriah, Abraham trusted God so completely that he was ready to do it — believing God would provide a way (Genesis 22). God stopped him and provided a ram instead.

(c) Personal example: I want to follow Abraham's example of trusting God when things are difficult — for example, when I am worried about exams, I will pray and give my best effort rather than panic, trusting that God will guide me. (1 mark)
2 marks for part (a) — 1 per promise stated clearly. 2 marks for part (b) — 0.5 for definition + 0.75 each example. 1 mark for part (c) — any sincere, connected personal application.
Two promises = descendants (stars) + land (Canaan). Abraham's faith = Leave without knowing + Sacrifice without understanding. God rewarded both.
Question 31 5 marks
Holy Orders and Religious Life: (a) Three degrees + one responsibility each. (b) Three vows of Religious priests explained in own words.
Model Answer
(a) Three Degrees of the Ministerial Priesthood: (3 marks)

1. Deacon: Assists the priest at Mass; baptises; leads funeral services; distributes Holy Communion; can witness marriages; serves the community especially the poor and needy.

2. Priest: Celebrates the Eucharist (Mass); administers the Sacraments (Baptism, Confession, Anointing of the Sick, Marriage); preaches the Word of God; pastors a parish community.

3. Bishop: Leads a diocese (a region of the Church); ordains priests and deacons; confirms Catholics; oversees all the parishes in his diocese; teaches and governs the local Church.

(b) Three Vows of Religious Priests: (2 marks)

1. Poverty: The religious priest gives up owning personal property or wealth. Everything belongs to the community. This frees him to serve God without being attached to money or things.

2. Obedience: He promises to obey his superiors — the head of his religious community and ultimately the Pope. He places his will under God's will as expressed through his leaders.

3. Chastity: He gives up marriage and family life, and lives celibately, so that he can give his whole heart and energy to God and to the people he serves.
Part (a): 1 mark per degree — 0.5 for naming, 0.5 for responsibility. Part (b): 2 marks for all 3 vows — award if student can name + explain the meaning in own words. Accept reasonable explanations.
Diocesan priests promise 2 things: obedience + celibacy. Religious priests vow 3 things: poverty + obedience + chastity. The extra one for religious is POVERTY.
Question 32 5 marks
Essay: Choose ONE commandment. Write about what it teaches, a Bible story/quote, and how you plan to live it as a Grade 7 student.
Model Answers (one per commandment choice)
Option A — 4th Commandment (Honour Your Parents):
The 4th commandment teaches us to love, respect and obey our parents and all those in authority over us. God gives us parents as the first teachers of faith and goodness. The Bible says, "Honour your father and your mother" (Exodus 20:12) and promises a long life to those who do so. As a Grade 7 student, I can live this commandment by listening to my parents without arguing, helping at home without being asked, and showing gratitude for everything they do for me.

Option B — 7th Commandment (Do Not Steal):
The 7th commandment teaches us to respect the property of others and to obtain goods only through just means such as working, buying or inheriting. Zacchaeus the tax collector (Luke 19:8) showed us how to make up for stealing — he promised to give back four times what he had taken. As a Grade 7 student, I can live this commandment by never taking anything from a classmate without permission, returning borrowed items, and being honest in shops.

Option C — 8th Commandment (Do Not Give False Witness):
The 8th commandment teaches us to be truthful in all we say and to protect the good name of others. Jesus said, "The truth will set you free" (John 8:32). The Catechism of the Catholic Church (CCC 2464) teaches that we are called to witness to the truth in every situation. As a Grade 7 student, I plan to live this by never telling lies about my friends, not spreading rumours, and being honest with teachers even if it means admitting a mistake.

Option D — 10th Commandment (Do Not Covet):
The 10th commandment teaches us to be content with what we have and not to burn with jealousy or greed for what belongs to others. Chief Seattle once said that all things are connected and that greed destroys this connection. The Catechism (CCC 2534) says that coveting leads us away from God. As a Grade 7 student, I can live this commandment by being thankful for what my family provides, being happy for friends when they receive gifts, and not feeling jealous of those who have more than me.
